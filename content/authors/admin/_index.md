I am a Ph.D. student in the [Department of Communicative Sciences and Disorders](https://steinhardt.nyu.edu/departments/communicative-sciences-and-disorders) at [New York University](https://www.nyu.edu/), working with [Professor Si On Yoon](https://wp.nyu.edu/commlab/team/) in the [Communication, Memory, and Mind (CoMM) Lab](https://wp.nyu.edu/commlab/).

My research interests lie at the intersection of psycholinguistics, memory, and social-pragmatic communication. I study how listeners use language and shared knowledge to manage uncertainty during conversation. I am particularly interested in how the informativeness of a speaker’s descriptions shapes listeners’ interpretations, and how listeners update their expectations about the speaker as the conversation unfolds.

Before joining NYU, I was a research assistant and lab manager in the [Brain and Language Processing Lab](https://sites.google.com/view/ntublplab) at [National Taiwan University](https://www.ntu.edu.tw/english/), working with [Professor Chia-Lin Charlene Lee](https://scholar.google.com.tw/citations?user=58CFn84AAAAJ&hl=zh-TW&oi=ao). My research there used EEG/ERP methods to investigate referential ambiguity, working memory, and hemispheric contributions to syntactic processing.

I received an M.A. in Linguistics and a B.A. in Economics from National Taiwan University, with additional training through the Neurobiology and Cognitive Science Program. My master’s thesis, *Processing of Ambiguity with Multiple Referents*, examined how people process ambiguous expressions when multiple potential referents are available and how working memory supports this process. Alongside my research, I have taught linguistics and psychology to high school students and mentored undergraduate and master’s students in experimental design and ERP data analysis.
